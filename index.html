Tuesday · lunch

Minced pork soboro don + a soft-boiled egg

Cook it in the morning, not at the last minute. Fifteen minutes: mince broken up in infused oil with grated ginger, then soy, mirin, a spoon of maple and a splash of dashi, simmered until it's glossy and nearly dry. Rice cooker on a timer. At quarter to three it's a three-minute reheat and everyone eats within five minutes of walking through the door.

🔁 Make it at 1.5× — this is the whole trick. Half goes straight into a container for Thursday's lunch, which then costs twelve minutes instead of a second cook.

Cucumber and cherry tomato on the side rather than a cooked vegetable — it's lunch, and dinner is carrying the veg tonight. Soft, mild, nothing to cut.

⚠️ Keep the smaller portions modest. They eat again at six, and this lands at a quarter to three — a full bowl now is exactly why the early sitting gets picked at.

Tuesday · dinner

Beef & chicken liver udon

Same beef and liver as last week, in a broth instead of over rice. Thin sliced beef, the liver in small seared pieces through it. Soak 150 g of livers in milk for 20 minutes and pour the milk away, cut to about a centimetre, sear 60–90 seconds and take them straight out. Back into the grown-ups' bowls at the very end.

Why a broth this time: it is the best dish there is for two sittings an hour apart. The pot sits at a bare simmer and nothing gets worse — no reheated beef, no dried-out rice. The beef goes into the simmering broth for 60–90 seconds per bowl, the udon takes two minutes. The late sitting is as good as the early one.

The base, made once: kombu and bonito dashi (or a clean packet one), soy, mirin, a little maple, a slice of ginger. ⚠️ Check the dashi packet — instant powder is where onion and garlic hide.

Build it onion-free — infused oil, plenty of ginger, green tops only. Anyone who wants onion gets it wilted into their own bowl, not the pot.

Two veg: 🥬 bok choy cooked in the broth, two minutes — this is the one time boiling greens is fine, because everything that leaches out ends up in the soup you drink · red capsicum roasted and laid on top, plus a few cherry tomatoes on the side.

🔁 Buy about 1.4× the beef and bag 250 g of it raw and separate — in case Wednesday falls through the way it did last week. If it doesn't, that bag goes to the freezer untouched. Raw sliced beef keeps two days, and Wednesday is exactly two days out.

All the liver goes tonight — it reheats badly, so there's no second sitting for it.

Thursday · dinner

Soy-ginger braised chicken thigh with carrot

The little one's night — chicken, braised soft, mildly seasoned. Bone-in thigh if you can get it; the braise is better for it and it shreds easily.

Method: infused oil and a lot of ginger, brown the thighs skin-side down, then dashi or water with soy, mirin and a spoon of maple, carrots in chunks, lid on, 25–30 minutes. It sits on the stove and improves through both sittings — no reheating, no timing.

✅ Nobody's coming over this week, so the no-frying rule is off for once. The braise still wins the two sittings on its own merits — but it's worth knowing you had the choice.

Two veg: 🥬 spinach with sesame — blanch 30 seconds, squeeze it dry, dress with toasted sesame, soy and a little maple. Cold, made in the afternoon, out of the way · red capsicum raw in strips on the table, which keeps more of the vitamin C than roasting does.

Cook extra thighs. Shredded chicken makes Saturday lunch free, and Saturday morning is gone.

Breakfasts

One filling thing + one protein — the same for both of them.

Rebuilt from scratch. The old version needed something done at 9pm and again at 6:30am, five days a week — so it didn't happen, and mornings defaulted to bought buns and cereal. Those are now the plan. Keep five things in the house and any morning sorts itself out with nothing but the microwave and the toaster.

Two rules, and they replace everything else.

① 🥚 The filling half is negotiable. The protein half is not. One from the second table, every morning.

② 🍬 Sweet fillings — two mornings a week between them. Everything else is free choice, including the same thing twice.
🫙 Overnight oats — batch four, never nightly. The word overnight made it sound like a job every evening. It isn't: four jars take six minutes and keep three or four days, so it's one job, twice a week, and then it's a grab item like everything else.

Per jar: ½ cup rolled oats · ½ cup milk · 2 tbsp yogurt · pinch of salt (a teaspoon of chia thickens it and adds fibre). Lid on, fridge, eight hours.

🍓 Toppings in the morning, not into the jar — banana, berries, freeze-dried fruit. Overnight they go soggy.
🥐 Croissants are a weekday item — baked the night before, kept airtight, ten seconds in the microwave if anyone wants them warm. The rule was never "no prep" — it's "no prep that feels like a chore." They still count against the two sweet mornings.
⛔ Nothing here needs a pan at 6:45am. Anything that does — scrambled eggs, fresh pancakes, prata — goes to the weekend. Night-before oven work is a different thing, and welcome.
🥚 Boiled eggs are the anchor now — everyone will eat them, so batch ten to twelve on Saturday night, not Sunday, and peel them. That single habit carries the protein half of the whole week.

Snack boxes

Three tubs, one from each. They pack it, not you.

No more assembling or baking. Three labelled tubs at a height they can reach, and the rule is one from each. Nothing is made — only picked. Packed by them, any time before bedtime.

🖨 There's a printed chooser chart for this now — laminated on the freezer. Ticked each week for what's in stock; they pick one from each colour.

❄️ Does it need the frozen cool bag? Default yes, every day — one habit beats one exception. Mornings here run past 30 °C and a school bag gets hotter than the room; ham and yogurt genuinely aren't safe warm for four hours.

✅ Any cheese, ham, yogurt, hummus or cut fruit → frozen bag, every time. No cheese is shelf-stable in this climate.

⚪ An all-dry box needs no cold at all — crackers, a milk box and a whole banana travel fine in any container. That's the answer on a night the bag didn't get refrozen, rather than a reason to skip the box.

🔁 The habit that makes it work: the bag goes into the freezer the moment it comes home, not into the sink. Give that to them alongside the packing — it's the step that quietly fails.

🧊 Two bags per child removes the problem for good; these need about twelve hours to refreeze, so a same-night turnaround is tight.
🔁 The same snack twice in a week is completely fine. There's deliberately no variety rule — the only cap is TREAT, two a week.

🧀 The cheese limit has been relaxed. The old "one day only" was a precaution, not a finding — the tracking points at refined carbs and dairy eaten in volume with nothing else, not at a cheese cube. Cheese two or three days a week is fine. What to avoid is stacking dairy across one day: if breakfast was milk or yogurt, send nuts, chickpeas or ham in the box instead.
